Sheet Size Guidance

You must assess the size and proportions of your own drawings and decide which paper format is appropriate. There is no single correct answer — the right sheet size depends on your house's footprint.

Too Small

  • A format too small for your plotted extents forces crammed-in, overlapping, or illegible drawings and dimensions

Too Large

  • An oversized format leaves a disproportionate amount of empty space around the drawings, weakening the presentation

Just Right

  • All three drawings — First Floor, Second Floor, Section/Elevation — sit on one sheet at 1/4" scale with balanced margins and no crowding
Common Sheet SizeTypical Fit
11″ × 17″ (ANSI B)Very compact house footprints only — rarely enough for two plans plus a section at 1/4"
18″ × 24″ (ARCH C)Typical fit for a modest 2,000–2,500 SF footprint across two plans plus a section
24″ × 36″ (ARCH D)Recommended default for most two-story houses in the 2,000–3,000 SF range
Lay out your three drawings at 1/4" scale in Model Space or on your viewport first, then choose the smallest sheet size that fits all three without crowding — that is your correct format.

Evaluation Rubric

Total possible score: 500 points across 4 criteria.

1. Architectural Program & Design Quality (150 pts)

Excellent
135–150 pts

The design fully satisfies the required program with logical adjacencies, efficient circulation, and a cohesive, original spatial concept. Total area falls within 2,000–3,000 SF.

Proficient
113–134 pts

All required rooms are present and the design is original, but adjacencies or circulation show minor inefficiencies, or total square footage sits slightly outside the target range.

Developing
0–112 pts

Required rooms are missing, the design closely resembles an existing or reference house, or the square footage falls well outside the 2,000–3,000 SF range.

2. Technical Drafting Precision (150 pts)

Excellent
135–150 pts

Both floor plans are drawn with precise, closed polyline wall geometry, and doors/windows correctly sized and inserted on their proper layers using the course template.

Proficient
113–134 pts

Floor plans are complete and functional but contain minor precision issues, or minor layer misassignments.

Developing
0–112 pts

Significant dimensional errors, unclosed or unjoined polylines, missing doors or windows, or geometry drawn on incorrect layers.

3. Section/Elevation & Vertical Coordination (100 pts)

Excellent
90–100 pts

The section/elevation accurately cuts through the stair connecting both floors, correctly reflects floor-to-floor heights, and both plans align vertically. Walls, stair location, and structure are consistent between floors.

Proficient
75–89 pts

Section is present and cuts through the stair, but shows minor inconsistencies in floor height or vertical alignment with the plans.

Developing
0–74 pts

Section/elevation is missing, does not cut through the stair, or the two floors do not align vertically with one another.

4. Sheet Layout, Plotting & Presentation (100 pts)

Excellent
90–100 pts

All three drawings — First Floor, Second Floor, Section/Elevation — are composed on a single, appropriately sized sheet at 1/4"=1'-0" with no crowding or excessive empty space. The provided CTB file and course template are applied correctly. PDF and physical printout are cleanly plotted at true scale.

Proficient
75–89 pts

All three drawings are on one sheet at the correct scale, but paper size selection is slightly mismatched — minor crowding or excess white space — or minor CTB/line weight inconsistencies exist.

Developing
0–74 pts

Drawings are split across multiple sheets, plotted at the wrong scale, paper size is clearly inadequate (crammed or disproportionately empty), or the CTB/template were not used.
