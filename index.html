CTD 330 AutoCAD for Interior Design — The University of Alabama
Course Overview

From precise digital geometry to production-ready construction documents.

CTD 330 builds AutoCAD fluency for interior design in a deliberate sequence — starting with exact coordinate drafting and layer discipline, moving through space planning, FF&E block design, and modular/reference workflows, and ending in scaled, annotated, plot-ready documentation. Each assignment below is a self-contained interactive guide with its own requirements, checklist, and rubric.

Lecture Quiz Policy

  • Quizzes are administered on Blackboard at the end of every lecture, covering that lecture's content
  • Each quiz allows a single attempt — there are no retakes
  • No screen devices are permitted during quiz time; students must take notes by hand in a notebook during lecture
  • Quizzes are open-note — students may reference their handwritten notes while answering

Student Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

01

Digital Drafting Precision

Construct dimensionally accurate 2D geometry using coordinate input, object snaps, and closed polylines, verifying geometric integrity.

02

CAD Information Management

Structure drawing data through disciplined layer systems, linetypes, lineweights, plot-style tables, and filter groups that conform to professional documentation conventions.

03

Spatial Construction & Space Planning

Generate and refine floor-plan geometry driven by area, dimensional, and adjacency constraints, using modify tools to resolve intersections.

04

Human Factors & Accessibility in Documentation

Model furniture, fixtures, and circulation to real-world ergonomic dimensions and required clearance/egress paths, embedding accessibility standards into drawn geometry.

05

FF&E Representation & Specification

Design and deploy component blocks that represent furnishings, appliances, and fixtures at appropriate levels of detail, supporting selection and specification intent.

06

Modular & Reference-Based Workflows

Build nested/modular assemblies and manage shared base files through blocks, in-place editing, and external references with correct origin and scale alignment.

07

Construction Documentation & Output

Translate model geometry into scaled paper-space layouts with correct viewports, title blocks, line-weight control, and true-scale PDF/plot output.

08

Annotation & Multi-Scale Communication

Apply scale-specific dimension, leader, and text systems to communicate measurements, callouts, and specifications legibly and consistently across drawing scales and audiences.

Course Objectives

The habits of mind and professional practices this course is designed to build:

  1. Precision Drafting & Coordinate Control — Build the discipline of drawing to exact measurements rather than by eye, using coordinate input and object snaps so geometry is dimensionally reliable and repeatable.
  2. Closed-Geometry Thinking — Treat linework as intentional, enclosed boundaries, developing the habit of verifying closure that underlies accurate areas, clean hatches, and editable shapes.
  3. Organizing Information with Layers — Develop a systems mindset: separating drawing content by purpose so information stays legible, controllable, and standards-compliant as complexity grows.
  4. Communicating Materials & Hierarchy Visually — Convey surfaces, cuts, and depth through hatching and draw order, building judgment about how a drawing reads at a glance.
  5. Constructing & Editing Complex Geometry — Fluency with modify tools, learning to derive forms from constraints (area, width, offset) and refine intersections rather than redrawing from scratch.
  6. Reading & Controlling Line Language — Build sensitivity to how linetypes and scale communicate meaning, learning that visual conventions are a language you actively manage, not a default.
  7. Working with Reusable Components — Develop the efficiency mindset of building once and deploying many times, learning why standardized blocks accelerate and stabilize professional work.
  8. Designing to Human & Ergonomic Standards — Internalize that geometry must serve real bodies and clearances, connecting drafting decisions to accessibility, dimensions, and functional intent.
  9. Managing Modularity & Nesting — Think in assembly hierarchies, understanding how changes propagate so you can update an entire project by editing one source.
  10. Producing Professional Documents — Acquire the output discipline that turns models into scaled, plottable deliverables, learning that a drawing isn't finished until it prints correctly and consistently.
  11. Annotating for Communication — Making drawings speak to others through scaled dimensions, callouts, and notes that stay legible and coordinated across views.
  12. Collaborating Through Referenced Files — Develop a professional workflow for sharing clean, aligned base files, and learn the file-management habits that keep team-based projects stable.
